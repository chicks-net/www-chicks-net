December closed out
[with nine releases](/posts/2025-12-31-github-recap-2025-12/); January
opened with something even better: a month where every single pull
request merged. One hundred and thirty opened, one hundred and thirty
merged. Either I was right about everything or nobody was arguing. Let
the record show I'm choosing to interpret this as competence.

The busiest repos were `template-repo` (29 — the gh-process machinery
grinding forward), `data-curated` (18), and `macaw` (15 — more on the
new bird in a moment). Note the reviews-given row still reads zero in
January; by summer I'd be reviewing a hundred-plus PRs a month. The
robot-review workflow that explains that jump has its origin story
*right here in this month's notable PRs*.

## Elsewhere

This is the part of January I'm most pleased about: **five pull requests
into [DNSControl/dnscontrol](https://github.com/DNSControl/dnscontrol),
all merged.** The DigitalOcean provider got self-documenting `Cannot()`
constructions for unsupported features, a corrected SOA support story
(disabled, then actually supported — the honest fix, in public), an
updated provider feature table, and a move of OWNERS to a proper
`.github/CODEOWNERS` that generated a ten-comment discussion — the
most-reviewed thread of my month, and every comment a human. In a
different repo, with different maintainers, being right isn't enough;
you also have to be agreed with. They agreed. It's a good feeling.

## Notable Pull Requests

### [www-chicks-net #270: Zip is the King of Stealth File Formats](https://github.com/chicks-net/www-chicks-net/pull/270)

1,986 additions and 1,199 deletions — the single biggest PR of the
month and, I'm fairly sure, the biggest blog post I've ever shipped.
[Zip Is King](/posts/2026-01-09-zip-is-the-king-of-stealth/) went up
January 9 and then kept growing: a table of contents, a note that
tar.gz is worse for developers (thanks, Lauren), and the genuinely
delightful discovery that PyTorch packages are just zip files (thanks,
Philip Brown). Three thousand changed lines for one blog post. The
comments thread even had humans in it — a collector's item.

### [template-repo #88: gh-process v5.4 applies copilot suggestions interactively](https://github.com/fini-net/template-repo/pull/88)

334 additions building the command that made AI review suggestions
something you apply deliberately, one at a time, with judgment —
instead of either ignoring them or cargo-culting the whole batch. This
is the PR that started the workflow that would have me "reviewing"
over a hundred PRs a month by July. I drew the line at *interactive*
and I stand by it.

### [chicks-home #57: opencode moves in](https://github.com/chicks-net/chicks-home/pull/57)

239 additions installing OpenCode in the home repo — the month a
second AI tooling system earned a place in the workflow. My home
directory now has more onboarding documentation than some companies I
have worked for.

### [homebrew-chicks #6: brew tests](https://github.com/chicks-net/homebrew-chicks/pull/6)

54 additions adding `brew` test coverage to the fresh tap, because a
package manager repo that ships formulae it never tested is just
confidence with a YAML extension.
