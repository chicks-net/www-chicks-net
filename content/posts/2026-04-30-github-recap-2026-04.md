[March launched gh-observer](/posts/2026-03-31-github-recap-2026-03/);
April cleaned up after the party — and then, just to keep things
interesting, landed a pull request in Hugo itself. The same Hugo that
builds the site you're reading. Some months the recursion comes to you.

Second perfect merge month of the year — January went 130 for 130,
April went 123 for 123. At this rate I'm either getting better at
planning PRs or the reviewers are getting soft, and since most of the
reviewers are robots I'm going to say it's the planning. The busiest
repo was `gh-observer` with 38 PRs in its post-launch polish phase.

## Elsewhere

The upstream PR of the month goes to
[Hugo itself](https://github.com/gohugoio/hugo/pull/14720): fixing
broken links in the top-level README of the static site generator that
has been building <https://www.chicks.net> since the beginning. Merged,
with a lively five-comment review thread of actual humans. There is
something deeply satisfying about fixing a paper cut in a tool you use
every single day, in public, for free, and having the maintainers say
yes.

## Releases Shipped

### gh-observer v1.8 through v2.1

Four releases in six days —
[v1.8 and v1.9 on April 24](https://github.com/fini-net/gh-observer/releases),
[v2.0 on the 25th](https://github.com/fini-net/gh-observer/releases/tag/v2.0),
and [v2.1 on the 29th](https://github.com/fini-net/gh-observer/releases/tag/v2.1).
The big-ticket items: cosign release signing with provenance bundles,
least-privilege GitHub Actions permissions, and an OpenSSF best
practices badge campaign that dragged the whole README up to level 3.
The security work that would later flow into ctm got its rehearsal
here.

### gh-amp is born

[gh-amp v0.1](https://github.com/fini-net/gh-amp/releases/tag/v0.1)
shipped April 19 — the gh extension for wrangling Copilot review
suggestions, born directly out of the copilot-review workflow that has
been growing in template-repo since January. When your problem is "the
robot reviewer is too efficient," you build a tool, not a complaint.

## Notable Pull Requests

### [www-chicks-net #318: my first 8 months with AI](https://github.com/chicks-net/www-chicks-net/pull/318)

2,160 additions and 685 deletions — the biggest blog PR of the year to
date — for
[My first 8 months with AI: the good, the bad, and the ugly](/posts/2026-04-18-8-months-with-AI/).
An honest accounting of what the AI-assisted workflow actually changed:
what it's good at, what it's terrible at, and why I keep the robots
around anyway. The follow-up PR cross-posting it to LinkedIn and
noting the hugo update felt thematically appropriate — the post about
AI tooling, promoted using AI tooling, on a site built by Hugo, days
before merging a PR into Hugo. It all runs together after a while.

### [chicks-home #68: daily_desktop_cleanup learns launchd](https://github.com/chicks-net/chicks-home/pull/68)

588 additions turning the desktop cleanup scripts into proper launchd
jobs with just recipes to manage them, plus the month's most-discussed
thread (ten comments, spread across the debug follow-ups — logs in
the *proper* place, timestamps, the whole debugging journey, in
public). Personal automation with production engineering standards,
because if it's worth automating it's worth automating
embarrassingly well.

### [gh-observer #222: release signing and provenance](https://github.com/fini-net/gh-observer/pull/222)

76 careful additions wiring up cosign signing and provenance bundles
for the release artifacts. Small PR, big deal: it's the difference
between "download this binary and trust me" and "verify this binary
and trust math."

### [www-chicks-net #315: the weather refund form](https://github.com/chicks-net/www-chicks-net/pull/315)

The
[weather refund form post](/posts/2026-04-01-weather-refund-form/)
landed April 1 with a straight face and a perfectly absurd premise.
Eighteen additions, nine deletions, and one deadpan joke sustained
across an entire blog post. Comedy is just engineering with worse
acceptance criteria.
