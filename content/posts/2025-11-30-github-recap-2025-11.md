[October built a CDN in twenty-five comments](/posts/2025-10-31-github-recap-2025-10/).
November published the follow-up: "digital ocean CDN is not awesome."
Six lines changed, eight more comments, and honestly the most
admirable engineering of the month — knowing when to walk something
back. Infrastructure months have sequels now.

## Elsewhere

One PR outside the family, and it's a character study:

## Notable Pull Requests

### [fini-infra #54: the CDN is not awesome](https://github.com/fini-net/fini-infra/pull/54)

Six additions, one deletion, eight comments — the shortest PR with
the longest title-to-diff ratio of the fall. This is the reversal of
October's 25-comment CDN build: after living with it, the verdict came
in, and the verdict was "not awesome." Engineering history is written
by the people willing to write the retraction.

### [fini-infra #57: vccinc.net on the DigitalOcean app platform](https://github.com/fini-net/fini-infra/pull/57)

256 additions standing up `vccinc.net` on DigitalOcean's app platform,
with `perlclass.org` as the primary domain and a www alias — the
domain-trust sites getting real hosting after the CDN reconsidered its
life choices. Nine comments of back-and-forth, and the sites stand
because of it.

### [www-chicks-net #177: DEI equals American Values](https://github.com/chicks-net/www-chicks-net/pull/177)

1,571 additions and 109 deletions for the
[DEI equals American Values post](/posts/2025-11-20-dei-equals-american-values/) —
the most substantial blog PR of the month for the most
words-likely-to-be-misunderstood post of the year. Plus a retagging
pass on the older Microsoft monopoly report, because posts about
monopolies and posts about values turn out to share a shelf.

### [mythical_person_month: the book learns to illustrate](https://github.com/chicks-net/mythical_person_month)

The Mythical Man-Month project rolled on — seventeen more PRs this
month, now extracting and documenting the chapter illustrations, and
finding modern links for the ancient references, which for a 1975 book
is basically archaeology with hyperlinks. Brooks cited things by
publication; the web needs URLs; the book now has both.
