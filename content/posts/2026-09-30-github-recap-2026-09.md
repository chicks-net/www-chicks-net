Back when I got started in open source, you mailed a patch to a mailing
list, waited a week, and thanked your lucky stars if a human read it. In
September 2026 I opened 200 pull requests, and I'm pretty sure I never
waited a week for anything. Times change. Some of those PRs were even
interesting, and one of them became a whole new project.

This is another in a series of monthly recaps of my public GitHub
activity — numbers first, with the stories behind the interesting bits.
The series started with [September 2025](/posts/2025-09-30-github-recap-2025-09/),
back when the robots weren't reviewing anything yet. They review
everything now. Progress, I guess.

Yes, two hundred PRs in one month. Before you picture me as some kind of
productivity demigod: 34 of those were the data-curated repo filing its
daily "Update GitHub data" paperwork all by itself, the way it does. The
robots are tireless and I am merely their supervisor. The other 166 were
human-shaped — well, human-supervised — and 195 of the 200 merged, which
either means my ideas were good or that nobody was standing in the way.
History will decide.

The busiest repos were the brand-new `ctm` (27 PRs), `template-repo` (18),
and `gh-observer` (17), with this very website pulling in a respectable
11. Thirty repos touched in a month is a lot of context switching, which
is either a sign of broad curiosity or an inability to focus. I've decided
to call it curiosity.

## Releases Shipped

### ctm v0.1 and v0.2

The big story of the month:
[ctm](https://github.com/chicks-net/ctm) — "Control Time Machines" — a Go
CLI for bossing around the [Time Machines Corp](https://timemachinescorp.com)
LED clocks over UDP: status, timers, colors, dimming, the works. v0.1
[shipped September 10](https://github.com/chicks-net/ctm/releases/tag/v0.1)
with the whole launch checklist — ffcli subcommands, unit tests with an
injectable dialer, a modular justfile, and the full quality/security
workflow suite I've been refining across the fleet. Two days later,
[v0.2](https://github.com/chicks-net/ctm/releases/tag/v0.2) added a
`local-test` recipe for sanity checks against real clocks and, more
importantly, a release pipeline that ships cosign-signed binaries with
SLSA build provenance. A project that went from nothing to signed,
provenance-attested releases in ten days is a good month by any measure.

### gh-observer v4.0

[gh-observer v4.0](https://github.com/fini-net/gh-observer/releases/tag/v4.0)
(September 13) was mostly a maintenance release — Go module updates and
the usual dependency parade from renovate and dependabot. The interesting
gh-observer work landed after the tag, and you'll find it below in the
notable PRs.

## Notable Pull Requests

### [ctm #44: release workflow with signed binaries and SLSA provenance](https://github.com/chicks-net/ctm/pull/44)

This one earned its spot the old-fashioned way: multiple review cycles,
one evening, no mercy. 339 additions of release pipeline, reviewed and
re-reviewed four times between 9pm and midnight — each round a fresh
review, my point-by-point responses ("Round 2 review — point by point"),
and a fix. The back-and-forth caught a real guard-rail bug in the version
regex and the injection-hardening around `$rel_version`. The result was
worth it: keyless cosign signing, SLSA provenance, and binaries you can
actually verify.

### [ctm #32 and #33: the CLI grows its spine](https://github.com/chicks-net/ctm/pull/32)

The feature work that made ctm a real tool rather than a script with
ideas above its station. [#32](https://github.com/chicks-net/ctm/pull/32)
(192 additions) modernized the CLI parsing to ffcli subcommands with
proper flags, and [#33](https://github.com/chicks-net/ctm/pull/33) added
the downtimer subcommands and the API 2.0 status decode — the actual
clock-wrangling functionality. From here, everything else in the project
was polish.

### [gh-observer #480: GitHub Enterprise URL support](https://github.com/fini-net/gh-observer/pull/480)

The biggest diff of the month by a comfortable margin: 1,183 additions and
352 deletions to teach gh-observer to talk to GitHub Enterprise servers.
That's a feature people actually ask for — plenty of shops live behind a
GHE fence and were previously stuck watching CI the old-fashioned,
miserable way. Now they can be miserable slightly less.

### [gh-amp #41: harden untrusted-input parsing, add fuzz targets](https://github.com/fini-net/gh-amp/pull/41)

807 additions of defensive engineering: hardened parsing of untrusted
input, function tests, and fuzz targets for the parsers. The PR that
reminds you that tools that read other tools' output need to be
suspicious of everything. A security-minded reviewer (GitHub's Advanced
Security bot) showed up for this one too, which feels appropriate.

Honorable mention to
[template-repo #352](https://github.com/fini-net/template-repo/pull/352),
the gh-process v9.1 security hardening work that generated seven comments
of lively discussion — it just lost the cut to the four above.
